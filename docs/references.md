# References and Data Sources

## Canonical dataset

Landry, N. (2026). *email-enron* (version 0.1) [Data set]. Zenodo. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21909507

The record describes a temporal hypergraph in which nodes are Enron email addresses and each hyperedge contains a sender and all recipients of an email event.

## Original Enron communication data

The Zenodo record identifies the historical Enron email dataset as its original source and notes that the corpus was made public during the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission investigation and later underwent correction and cleaning.

## Higher-order network reference

Benson, A. R., Abebe, R., Schaub, M. T., Jadbabaie, A., & Kleinberg, J. (2018). Simplicial closure and higher-order link prediction. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 115*(48), E11221 to E11230. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1800683115

The Zenodo record requests citation of this work for the higher-order email-network lineage.

## Network-efficiency reference

Latora, V., & Marchiori, M. (2001). Efficient behavior of small-world networks. *Physical Review Letters, 87*(19), 198701. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.87.198701

This paper introduces network efficiency as a measure based on inverse shortest-path distances and motivates the released global-efficiency operationalization.

## Software / data ecosystem

XGI documentation provides the email-enron dataset through the XGI data collection and independently reports 148 nodes and 10,885 hyperedges for the released dataset.

## How references are used

The dataset source defines the empirical evidence.

Latora and Marchiori motivate the efficiency metric.

The present repository contributes a secondary stress-test analysis and bounded Learning and Development interpretation. It does not claim invention of organizational network analysis, global efficiency, or the Enron dataset.
